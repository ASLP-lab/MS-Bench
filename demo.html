Overview

Abstract

Multi-speaker automatic speech recognition (MSASR) has advanced rapidly with end-to-end speech large language models. However, existing benchmarks cover limited ranges of specific multi-speaker conditions and scenarios, making it difficult to reveal system limitations under diverse conditions and scenarios. We introduce MS-Bench, a condition-stratified benchmark for fine-grained MSASR evaluation. MS-Bench integrates both public and internal benchmarks and characterizes each recording along five dimensions: speaker number, overlap ratio, speaker similarity, speaker turn interval, and acoustic quality. We stratify these dimensions into condition ranges and construct MS-Bench with broad coverage of conditions, scenarios, languages, and recording devices. Evaluating representative commercial, open-source, and cascaded systems, we find that overlap affects both lexical recognition and speaker attribution, while other factors exhibit more task-specific effects on recognition, attribution, or temporal prediction. These results demonstrate that aggregate metrics alone obscure important MSASR failure modes and highlight the value of condition-stratified evaluation. MS-Bench aims to reveal condition-specific limitations of current MSASR systems and guide future improvements.

Overall performance

Note: Seven systems from the paper. Lower is better for every error metric. DER uses a 0.5-second collar; tcpWER uses a 5-second collar.

Accuracy across tasks. MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize-Pro has the lowest error on all four metrics. Similar diarization scores can still hide large transcription differences: Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash and pyannote+Qwen3-ASR have DERs of 17.97% and 17.35%, but cpWERs of 29.43% and 43.25%.

Accuracy and robustness. MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize and the cascaded system have no excluded inference units. MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize-Pro has 15 excluded units despite its lower error rates, so accuracy and output robustness should be read together.

Metric and scoring protocol

DER: diarization error with a 0.5-second collar. cpWER: speaker-attributed transcription error under optimal speaker permutation. tcpWER: time-constrained cpWER with a 5-second collar. WER: speaker-agnostic lexical error.

Metrics use each system's scoreable inference units, so valid subsets may differ. Excluded units cannot be scored reliably. Soft-degradation units remain parseable and are retained.
